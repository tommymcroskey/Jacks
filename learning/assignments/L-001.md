## Briefing for Tommy

We want to explain what an account is worth at a particular point in the event
history. Cash is money available in the account. A position is the number of asset
units it holds. A mark is the currently available price used to value those units.
Equity is the total account value: cash plus the position's marked value.

This matters because later research will use account values to assess results.
A correct value does not by itself tell us profit: deposits can increase account
value without the investments earning anything. The ledger will separately track
deposits, withdrawals, and the opening value to explain that difference. Your
function supplies one trusted value that explanation needs.

### Accepted behavior and scaffold interface

- Input: cash in minor currency units, held quantity in whole asset units, and an
  optional current mark in minor currency units per asset unit. Use signed 64-bit
  integers; one CU equals 100 minor units in our fictional currency.
- Valid domain: nonnegative cash and quantity; a present mark is nonnegative.
  Validate a supplied negative mark even when holdings are zero. A zero mark is
  valid and must not be confused with an absent mark.
- Output: either nonnegative equity in minor units or one error: `invalid_input`,
  `missing_mark`, or `overflow`. The scaffold declares `jacks::calculate_equity`
  and `ValuationResult` (`std::variant<Equity, ErrorCode>`) in
  [valuation.hpp](../../include/jacks/valuation.hpp); exact API review is pending.
- Error precedence: invalid signed inputs first; then missing mark for positive
  holdings; then unrepresentable multiplication or addition. No partial numeric
  result on failure. Arithmetic must be checked before a signed operation could
  overflow; signed overflow has undefined behavior in C++.
- With zero holdings, an absent mark is allowed and equity is cash. With positive
  holdings, absence prevents valuation. The caller supplies the latest mark that
  was actually available; this function cannot verify the history of that price.

Use integer minor units to avoid an initial rounding policy. Fractional quantities
or floating-point prices need a later contract change. This function does not
update cash/holdings, process trades, calculate profit/returns, validate event
order, parse files, fetch prices, or convert currencies. Fees are already reflected
in snapshot cash; do not subtract them again.

## Acceptance and review method

- Results match independent expected fixtures, including cash-only, a positive
  holding, a present zero mark, and a missing mark with positive holdings.
- Reject negative inputs and demonstrate both multiplication and addition
  overflow boundaries, including an exactly representable boundary value.
- Inputs remain unchanged. Explain units, missing versus zero mark, and why this
  result is equity rather than profit.
- Supply focused tests with independently justified expected values and exact
  build/test commands. A peer runs them on the submitted commit. Passing tests
  alone does not establish a profitable strategy or full ledger correctness.
- Review comments distinguish correctness, interface, maintainability, and
  preference. Revise substantive findings; mentor records demonstrated learning
  from your explanation and changes, not an assumed skill score.
